# Contrast administration and phase: evidence needed to define LDCT claims

_Updated 2026-09-14 after reviewing the note introduced at `e7efa20` and the ingestion code at `646daa6c3a01ab807dc729982115c57bdec3ff3a`. Companion: [what the field needs solved](FIELD_COLLAPSE_PROBLEMS.md)._

**Contrast administration and phase belong in the scope of a dose–task claim, but one agent-name field cannot establish phase.** The current harmonized metadata omits these factors. That omission must be distinguished from missing knowledge about the source collection: the AAPM 2016 challenge has published portal-venous protocol provenance, while LIDC cannot be assumed to be uniformly unenhanced.

This correction retains the original note's scope concern and replaces its unsupported recommendation to declare an unenhanced-only field. It changes no dataset, ingestion code, scoring rule, tolerance, release permission or owner decision. It assigns no new data collection and declares no experimental requirement solved.

## 1. Why the scope matters

The committed [task specification](WS-4_leaderboard/scoring/task_spec.py) uses a signal-known-exactly Gaussian lesion with 20 HU positive peak contrast and sigma 2 pixels, with an ROI selection band of [10, 120] HU. This is a declared synthetic task; the HU band is not an organ segmentation or a contrast-phase classifier. It cannot establish whether selected tissue is enhanced, and neither that task nor a high-frequency-energy ratio establishes clinical liver-lesion performance.

The relevant clinical protocol must therefore accompany any proposed dose frontier: anatomy, task, contrast administration, route and phase when applicable, acquisition and reconstruction settings, and dose-generation method. Different enhancement patterns may change lesion conspicuity and background structure, but this note supplies no measured effect size, fixed tissue-HU threshold or universal spatial-frequency interpretation. Whether a result transfers between specified protocols remains an empirical question under [the outcome requirements](FIELD_COLLAPSE_PROBLEMS.md).

## 2. What is known, and what the repository does not preserve

### AAPM: known collection protocol, incomplete harmonized provenance

AAPM describes its 2016 challenge patient library as abdominal CT with iodinated contrast, acquired 70 seconds after injection in the portal-venous phase. It also specifies automated tube potential and exposure selection, illustrating why contrast timing is part of a defined acquisition protocol. The source provides paired full-dose and quarter-dose training data. Its patient quarter-dose data were generated by noise insertion, not a separate physical reduced-dose acquisition. These are collection-level facts, not conclusions from inspecting local headers. Sources: [AAPM challenge patient-data description](https://www.aapm.org/GrandChallenge/LowDoseCT/) and [AAPM challenge flyer](https://www.aapm.org/grandchallenge/lowdosect/documents/RSNAGrandChallengeFlyer.pdf).

The [committed cross-group summary](WS-1_dataset/output/aapm_lidc_cross_vendor_spread_summary.md) explicitly identifies its AAPM arm as AAPM 2016, and [the data plan](WS-1_dataset/data_needs.md) records that source as already staged. Thus the earlier statement that the project has no enhanced paired-dose data is incorrect if it refers to these noise-inserted pairs. This does not establish independent physical low-dose validation or coverage of other phases. Confirming the exact evaluated series' membership in the documented collection and carrying that source attribution into harmonized records remain provenance work; this review did not inspect those series.

### LIDC: source identity does not prove an unenhanced scan

The original LIDC investigators describe both screening and diagnostic examinations, acquired under participating institutions' protocols. Their early 30-scan study included intravenous contrast: only 20 of the 30 scans were reported as unenhanced. This refutes a blanket claim that LIDC is unenhanced by protocol. Those early counts are not a contrast inventory of the complete LIDC-IDRI release or of this project's selected patients. Source: [Armato et al., LIDC radiologist-variability study, Patient image data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2290739/).

Neither a vendor name nor a lung-screening description in a project README establishes an individual scan's administration status or phase. In particular, there is no verified basis here for asserting that the two patients within each LIDC vendor group share a phase. These records require source-specific evidence or an explicit unknown status.

### Harmonized metadata: the omission is visible in code

The [DICOM mapping](WS-1_dataset/schema/dicom_to_hdf5_mapping.md) and [dataset schema](WS-1_dataset/schema/dataset_schema.md) have no contrast-administration or phase fields. The omission also exists in the actual path: [`read_ct_volume`](WS-1_dataset/pipelines/pwm_ldct_prep/adapters/base.py) extracts a fixed acquisition dictionary from a reference slice; [`build_metadata`](WS-1_dataset/pipelines/pwm_ldct_prep/harmonize.py) constructs another fixed acquisition dictionary for output. Neither preserves contrast metadata. [`validate_metadata`](WS-1_dataset/pwm_ldct_loader/src/pwm_ldct_loader/metadata.py) does not require or validate it.

This establishes a gap in the reviewed schema and implementation. It is not an inspection of every released artifact or proof that the original DICOM lacks the information. The existing cross-group comparison already mixes cohort, anatomy, vendor, protocol and dose-generation method. Contrast administration and phase are additional protocol details to record; the current comparison cannot isolate their causal effects or a vendor effect.

## 3. Correct DICOM identities and their limits

The earlier note misidentified `(0018,0012)` as the injection start time. The standard defines these attributes separately:

| Tag | DICOM keyword | Information supplied |
|---|---|---|
| `(0018,0010)` | `ContrastBolusAgent` | Agent description |
| `(0018,0012)` | `ContrastBolusAgentSequence` | Coded agent identification |
| `(0018,1040)` | `ContrastBolusRoute` | Administration route |
| `(0018,1042)` | `ContrastBolusStartTime` | Injection start time |

An agent description identifies the agent, not the acquisition's contrast phase. Route and injection start time alone do not provide the image acquisition's delay or phase either. Source: [DICOM PS3.3, Contrast/Bolus Module](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.4.html).

For applicable enhanced objects, the standard provides per-frame contrast information in `ContrastBolusUsageSequence` `(0018,9341)`: `ContrastBolusAgentAdministered` `(0018,9342)`, `ContrastBolusAgentDetected` `(0018,9343)`, and `ContrastBolusAgentPhase` `(0018,9344)`. Its phase vocabulary includes pre-contrast, arterial, venous, portal-venous and delayed states. These attributes distinguish administration from detection and nominal phase; their availability is not guaranteed in the classic CT series this pipeline reads. Source: [DICOM PS3.3, Contrast/Bolus Usage Macro, §C.7.6.16.2.12](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.16.2.html#sect_C.7.6.16.2.12).

**Missing, empty, removed during de-identification, and confirmed unenhanced are different states.** A nonempty agent field is not sufficient to classify a pre-contrast versus post-contrast series. An oral agent must not automatically become evidence of intravenous enhancement. Any timing-based inference needs acquisition timing, consistent temporal handling and a validated interpretation for the protocol; it must be labelled as inferred rather than directly recorded phase.

## 4. Proposed provenance work for review

The useful next step is an evidence inventory for existing data, followed by a reviewed metadata design. A proposed record should distinguish:

- Administration status and route, retaining unknown or conflicting evidence explicitly.
- Phase when supported, separately from whether any contrast was administered; include unknown and not-applicable states with defined meanings.
- Evidence origin and granularity: a collection protocol declaration, source series/frame metadata, or documented curator adjudication. Collection-level attribution must not be presented as a tag measured in each local series.
- The source version, affected harmonized series and review disposition, with coverage and missingness counts. Do not silently assign one frame's value to a heterogeneous series.

The data holder can first verify source membership and source documentation without moving raw headers or pixels. Where permitted source metadata is needed, interpretation belongs at the controlled source location. A normalized, reviewed descriptor is preferable to publishing arbitrary free text. This note does not authorize retaining new raw fields or publishing clock times: the current [cleaning specification](WS-1_dataset/schema/dicom_cleaning_spec.md) excludes these contrast tags from its whitelist and removes date/time values. The [cleaning implementation](WS-1_dataset/pipelines/pwm_ldct_prep/deident.py) likewise omits them. DICOM recognizes potential identifying information in dates/times and requires coherent temporal handling for analyses involving injection and acquisition timing. Source: [DICOM PS3.15, Retain Longitudinal Temporal Information Options](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part15/sect_E.3.6.html).

A later implementation must address extraction, harmonization, schema/versioning, validation, cleaning and existing-artifact migration together. Merely editing the mapping document or adding an agent attribute to the adapter would not make the current output carry phase. Synthetic metadata checks would need to cover missing/empty values, explicit negative evidence, route distinctions, conflicting series/frame information, and agent-present-but-phase-unknown cases. No such implementation or validation is claimed here.

## 5. Scope decisions follow the evidence

An unenhanced-only claim is possible only for an explicitly verified eligible subset, with the exclusion and its impact reported. The current source names and absent fields do not establish that subset, so this note does not narrow the program to it. Conversely, AAPM's documented portal-venous protocol does not support claims about all contrast-enhanced CT, other phases, or independent physical dose reduction.

For any chosen claim, report contrast administration and phase alongside the task, population, acquisition, uncertainty and comparator requirements in [FIELD_COLLAPSE_PROBLEMS.md](FIELD_COLLAPSE_PROBLEMS.md). Report unknown and uncovered strata, and preserve limited or inconclusive results where sample sizes cannot support a phase-specific conclusion. Existing small groups do not acquire adequate statistical power merely by adding a metadata field.

**Completion evidence for this gap:** a traceable inventory of existing source evidence; a reviewed schema and functioning propagation/validation path; per-record status with explicit unknowns and evidence origin; and a declared claim boundary consistent with supported strata. A clinical conclusion additionally needs the task-specific validation already required by the main problem document. Recording phase alone neither validates a method nor guarantees closure of the LDCT field.
