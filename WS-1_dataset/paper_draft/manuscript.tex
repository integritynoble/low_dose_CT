\begin{abstract}
\noindent LIDC-IDRI, the most widely used public thoracic CT collection, anchors a large fraction of low-dose CT reconstruction research, yet it is distributed in a format-specific layout with heterogeneous annotation conventions and no content-addressed versioning. We present PWM-LDCT v0.5, a unified, content-addressed harmonization of the LIDC-IDRI collection as a first release. As new openly-licensed data records it deposits: harmonized multi-task annotations (majority-vote lung-nodule labels reused from the four-radiologist LIDC annotations, with the raw per-reader labels preserved); physically-grounded simulated low-dose images at dose ratios 0.10 / 0.25 / 0.50 for the full-dose-only scans, with the simulation discrepancy quantified in Technical Validation; harmonized per-scan metadata; and deterministic patient-level splits. These are delivered through a pip-installable Python loader and three Docker pipelines that deterministically regenerate the HDF5 shards from the source's raw distribution (verified against a released per-voxel tolerance), and demonstrated by baseline reproductions of four reconstruction methods. The release is identified by a SHA-256 manifest hash and deposited under a PhysioNet DOI with a mirrored Zenodo archive. The AAPM 2016 low-dose collection, which adds genuine paired-dose coverage, is planned as a 10-patient real-paired validation subset of the v1.0 roadmap and is not part of this v0.5 release.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Background \& Summary}

Public datasets have driven a decade of progress in deep-learning low-dose CT reconstruction~\citep{chen2017redcnn,wang2020deeplearningreview}. LIDC-IDRI~\citep{lidc_idri} (1{,}018 thoracic CT scans with four-radiologist nodule annotations; full-dose only) is the most widely used public thoracic CT collection and anchors a large fraction of the published evaluation literature. One further public low-dose CT collection --- the 2016 AAPM-Mayo Low-Dose CT Grand Challenge release~\citep{aapm2016challenge} (10 patients with real full-dose acquisitions and matched 25\%-dose data generated by Mayo's validated projection-domain noise-insertion technique, not a second physical scan) --- is planned for the PWM-LDCT v1.0 roadmap as a 10-patient real-paired validation subset rather than this first release, because it carries paired-dose coverage that requires additional licensing and harmonization work. The Mayo LDCT-and-Projection-Data collection~\citep{mccollough2020mayo} (299 patients extending AAPM 2016 with raw projection data; 199 publicly accessible, spanning GE and Siemens scanners) is retained as landscape context only and is not in the release scope. **Scope decision (2026-08-21): the v1.0 roadmap now adds only the AAPM 2016 collection (10 training patients, FD+QD paired) as a real-paired validation subset; the Mayo LDCT-PD collection and prospective multi-vendor acquisition are not in the release scope.** This v0.5 paper therefore focuses on the LIDC-IDRI collection alone.

Yet even this single, widely-used source is not designed to be used as a benchmark. Three structural gaps prevent the field from treating it as a coherent one:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Loader fragmentation.} LIDC-IDRI is distributed in its own format. Every published method that evaluates on it re-implements its own preprocessing, leading to silent format-conversion drift between papers. A method reporting PSNR on ``LIDC'' may have used any of half a dozen subtly-different pixel-spacing, HU-windowing, or slice-thickness conventions.
    \item \textbf{Annotation heterogeneity.} LIDC-IDRI ships four-radiologist nodule annotations in an XML format unique to the project. A method that wants to evaluate downstream-task performance on the full-dose images must reconcile this annotation convention with its own tooling; there is no shared, versioned majority-vote consolidation.
    \item \textbf{Benchmark drift.} LIDC-IDRI has no content-addressed version identifier. As papers are written, the dataset is silently re-cleaned, re-split, or sub-sampled; a result tied to ``LIDC-IDRI'' in one paper is not guaranteed to be evaluated against the same scans as a result tied to ``LIDC-IDRI'' in another paper a year later.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{PWM-LDCT v0.5} closes these three gaps by adding an annotation-and-infrastructure layer over the existing LIDC-IDRI collection rather than collecting new scans. Its \emph{new, openly-licensed data records} are (a) harmonized multi-task annotations --- majority-vote lung-nodule labels reused from the existing four-radiologist LIDC annotations, plus the raw per-reader labels behind each majority vote; (b) physically-grounded simulated low-dose images at dose ratios $r \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$ for the full-dose-only LIDC scans, whose simulation behavior is quantified in Technical Validation rather than asserted; and (c) harmonized per-scan metadata with deterministic patient-level splits. These records are delivered through (d) a unified Python loader that surfaces the LIDC collection under a single schema and (e) Docker preprocessing pipelines that deterministically regenerate the harmonized HDF5 shards from the source's raw distribution (reproducible within a released per-voxel tolerance), and they are version-pinned by (f) a content-addressed manifest identified by a SHA-256 hash that any downstream user can verify offline against a locally-built copy and that is deposited under a permanent PhysioNet DOI with a mirrored Zenodo archive. Baseline reproductions of four community-standard methods, reported in Technical Validation, demonstrate that the records support end-to-end training and evaluation without code modification. Table~\ref{tab:source_comparison} summarizes the source composition of v0.5 and the v1.0 roadmap.

The v0.5 paper's contribution is the new harmonized annotation and simulated-low-dose records, the unified loader and preprocessing layer, and the content-addressed manifest deposited under a PhysioNet DOI --- not the underlying scans. The scans themselves already exist in the cited source, and v0.5 does not redistribute them under a different license; downstream users still obtain access to each source through that source's own gatekeeper (NBIA for LIDC). What v0.5 \emph{deposits} is the value-added layer: the new annotation records, the simulated-low-dose images derived from the public full-dose scans, the harmonized metadata, and the code that deterministically reproduces the harmonized shards from the source within a released per-voxel tolerance.

All descriptor claims (the harmonization layer, content-addressing, annotation reuse, the loader, and baseline reproducibility) are self-contained; separate companion work on evaluation methodology and a community leaderboard is in preparation but is not required to obtain, use, or validate this release.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Ethics, consent, and data sharing}

PWM-LDCT v0.5 does not collect new data. All scans are sourced from the LIDC-IDRI public dataset, which was released under its institutional approval. Our use complies with the data-use agreement of the source:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{LIDC-IDRI}~\citep{lidc_idri}: CC BY 3.0 with attribution requirement; no redistribution restriction. PWM-LDCT v0.5 cites LIDC-IDRI per the attribution requirement and does not redistribute the underlying DICOM files; users download from NBIA directly.
\end{itemize}

The AAPM 2016 collection, planned as the v1.0 real-paired validation subset, will be used under its research-use license (Mayo Clinic data-sharing agreement); it is \textbf{not} part of v0.5. The Mayo LDCT-PD collection is not in the release scope (removed 2026-08-21).

No new scans are collected for v0.5, and the source dataset is already de-identified to HIPAA Safe Harbor standards by its original release team. No top-up annotation was required for v0.5 because the LIDC nodule task reuses the existing four-radiologist annotations; the top-up annotation planned for v1.0 (see ``Annotation harmonization'' below) would involve board-certified radiologists reading already-public, already-de-identified images and generating only image-level labels --- with no patient contact and no new protected health information. Because v0.5 involves only secondary analysis of already-public, already-de-identified images (see ``Ethics-exemption basis'' below), it does not require a new IRB determination or informed consent; compliance rests on the source data-use agreements (LIDC-IDRI CC BY 3.0; AAPM 2016 research DUA for the v1.0 validation subset). v0.5 is published under the PhysioNet model as an annotation-, metadata-, and code release; users obtain the underlying scans through the source's own access channel and then run our preprocessing pipeline locally.

\subsubsection*{Ethics-exemption basis for v0.5 (this release).}
The v0.5 release rests on a public-data compliance basis with three independent grounds, each documented in the source data-use agreements and the Supplementary Information: (i) \textbf{public-source data only} --- every scan is drawn from an existing public distribution (LIDC-IDRI via NBIA), which was not collected by or for the authors; (ii) \textbf{no PHI} --- the source dataset was de-identified by its original release team to HIPAA Safe Harbor standards, and we additionally re-verify de-identification with an automated DICOM-tag whitelist sweep plus an OCR pass over every reconstructed slice (see ``De-identification verification''), with no PHI detected; and (iii) \textbf{no human-subjects interaction} --- v0.5 involves only secondary analysis of already-de-identified images (annotation reuse), with no patient contact, no intervention, and no re-identification attempt; the resulting records contain no identifiers and carry no link to re-identifiable source data. Under 45~CFR~46, this activity is not research involving human subjects (secondary analysis of de-identified data) and is therefore not subject to the Common Rule's consent requirement; the compliance basis is documented for journal and repository (PhysioNet) review. The top-up annotation planned for v1.0 will follow the same basis (image-level labels from already-public, already-de-identified images) and will be documented separately.

\subsubsection*{Compliance for v1.0 (AAPM 2016 validation subset).}
The v1.0 AAPM 2016 validation subset involves \emph{no new acquisitions}: the 10 training patients were collected under the original Mayo Clinic research protocol and are distributed under the AAPM 2016 Grand Challenge research-use data-sharing agreement. Our use complies with that DUA; no IRB approval or informed-consent pathway is required because no new human-subjects data are collected. v0.5 is not gated on any v1.0 compliance step, and the two releases remain independent.

\subsection*{Source dataset selection}

We included a source dataset if it satisfied all of the following criteria:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Publicly distributed (free academic registration or research-DUA access; no proprietary or vendor-restricted access)
    \item DICOM-formatted scans with documented acquisition parameters
    \item Clear data-use agreement permitting research use and derived-artifact publication
    \item Active maintenance status at the source's listed repository as of 2026-06 (per the deposited \texttt{deident\_audit.jsonl} provenance timestamps)
\end{itemize}

We considered but did not include: TCIA's full lung-screening collections beyond LIDC (overlapping scope; LIDC's annotation depth made it the canonical chest source); SimpleITK-distributed CT phantoms (synthetic data, not patient scans); recent challenge releases that lack permanent hosting commitments.

\subsection*{Harmonization (v0.5: LIDC; AAPM 2016 per v1.0 validation subset)}

The unified PWM-LDCT schema applies the following transformations to the source's raw distribution, with the goal that downstream methods see a single consistent format; v0.5 implements the schema for the LIDC source, and the AAPM 2016 source is planned as the v1.0 real-paired validation subset under the same schema:

\paragraph{Hounsfield-unit calibration.} The LIDC source reports HU on the standard scale; we apply per-source HU offset corrections measured from the AAPM CT performance phantom data published with the source. Per-source HU offsets are recorded in \texttt{metadata.json} so methods that depend on the raw scanner HU values can opt out. For the v1.0 AAPM 2016 validation subset, per-source HU offsets will be recorded identically.

\paragraph{Pixel-spacing normalization.} The loader exposes an optional resampling to a common 0.75 mm in-plane spacing; the original spacing is preserved in metadata. Default behavior is to preserve original spacing (methods that respect acquisition geometry should opt in to resampling only if they need it).

\paragraph{Slice-thickness handling.} LIDC (v0.5) includes thin-slice ($\leq$ 1.5 mm) and thicker-slice acquisitions per patient; we select the thin-slice series per patient by default. The loader exposes a \texttt{slice\_thickness=} argument for users wanting other thickness choices. AAPM 2016 (v1.0 validation subset) also provides thin (1\,mm) series and will be handled identically.

\paragraph{Reconstruction-kernel preservation.} Each source uses different vendor-specific reconstruction kernels (LIDC in v0.5: heterogeneous across LIDC-contributing sites; v1.0 validation subset: vendor-standard Siemens kernels, B30f for AAPM 2016). We do \textbf{not} re-reconstruct to a common kernel because doing so would discard the very kernel-specific structure that downstream methods may wish to model. Per-scan kernel metadata is preserved in \texttt{metadata.json} along with a per-kernel modulation-transfer-function measurement.

\paragraph{Low-dose simulation harmonization.} For the LIDC scans (which are full-dose only), we provide simulated low-dose images at $r \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$ using a physically-grounded projection-domain forward model. The model is implemented in the released pipeline (\texttt{pwm\_ldct\_prep.lowdose\_sim}) and, where available, defers to the equivalent reference implementation in \texttt{pwm\_core.contrib.modalities.ct\_radon}~\citep{pwmcore}. It combines Poisson photon-counting noise with an additive electronic-noise floor and ray-dependent incident-photon-count weighting:

\begin{equation}
    N_r(\ell) \sim \textrm{Poisson}\!\big(I_0(\ell) \cdot r \cdot e^{-s_{\textrm{ref}}(\ell)}\big) + \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,\sigma_e^2\big), \qquad \tilde{s}_{\textrm{low}}(\ell) = -\ln\!\left( \frac{\max\!\big(N_r(\ell),\,1\big)}{I_0(\ell) \cdot r} \right)
    \label{eq:lowdose_sim}
\end{equation}

where $s_{\textrm{ref}}(\ell)$ is the full-dose effective-monochromatic log-attenuation projection along ray $\ell$ obtained by forward Radon transform of the reconstructed image; $I_0(\ell) = I_0^{(0)} \cdot b(\ell)$ is the ray-dependent incident-photon count combining a per-scanner monochromatic-equivalent reference $I_0^{(0)}$ (calibrated from manufacturer-published kVp / mAs / pitch tables) with the scanner's bowtie-filter profile $b(\ell)$ (calibrated from manufacturer-published or in-house phantom measurements); $\sigma_e$ is the per-scanner electronic-noise standard deviation, calibrated from low-flux phantom measurements~\citep{zeng2015nonlocal}; and $r$ is the dose-reduction ratio. The $\max(\cdot,1)$ guard prevents log-of-zero at extreme attenuations. In v0.5 the model is applied to the LIDC full-dose scans only; under the v1.0 roadmap the same forward model will be applied to the AAPM full-dose scans so the simulated low-dose distribution is consistent across sources, and the AAPM \textit{real} low-dose acquisition will be preserved alongside its simulated counterpart so users can directly compare.

\paragraph{Simulation approximations and known limitations.} Equation~\eqref{eq:lowdose_sim} is an effective-monochromatic forward model and does not capture all physical effects of polychromatic CT acquisition. We make four approximations explicit so users can judge applicability:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monochromatic approximation.} We use a kVp-equivalent effective mean photon energy rather than integrating over a polychromatic X-ray spectrum. This is the standard simplification in published LDCT simulators~\citep{zeng2015nonlocal,wang2020deeplearningreview}; it does not capture beam-hardening artifacts, which therefore appear with the same intensity in both simulated and full-dose images and are not differentially induced by dose reduction.
    \item \textbf{Bowtie + spectrum from manufacturer tables.} Where the source dataset's DICOM headers do not preserve the per-scan bowtie profile, we fall back to a uniform $b(\ell) \equiv 1$ and flag the affected scans in \texttt{metadata.json}; users can opt to drop these scans from a sensitivity analysis.
    \item \textbf{Detector quantum efficiency.} Detector QE is folded into the calibrated $I_0^{(0)}$ rather than modeled separately. This is appropriate when downstream evaluation uses an aggregate signal-to-noise metric; users who require explicit DQE modeling (e.g., for detector-comparison studies) should not use these simulated images.
    \item \textbf{Electronic-noise floor.} The Gaussian electronic-noise term $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_e^2)$ is calibrated globally per scanner rather than spatially-varying across the detector array. Position-dependent electronic noise (a $\leq$~5\,\% effect on typical clinical scanners) is not modeled.
\end{itemize}

The discrepancy between our simulated low-dose and a reference noise-inserted low-dose at $r = 0.25$ is planned for the v1.0 AAPM 2016 paired-dose cohort in the Technical Validation section (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_vs_real_v05}); note that such a reference would itself be produced by Mayo's validated projection-domain noise insertion, not a second physical low-dose acquisition. v0.5 contains no real paired-dose reference, so simulation-sensitivity users should consult the v1.0 analysis once available.

\subsection*{Annotation harmonization}

We provide two annotation classes under the 1.0 unified schema (lung-nodule detection and diagnostic-quality Likert), plus the QA protocol that produced them. In v0.5, the lung-nodule class is fully realized for LIDC (inherited four-radiologist annotations + majority-vote); the Likert class and the AAPM 2016 top-up annotations are v1.0 roadmap items:

\paragraph{Lung-nodule detection (chest scans).} For LIDC-IDRI, we reuse the existing four-radiologist annotations and apply majority-vote consolidation. The full 911-case LIDC \texttt{raw\_per\_reader} release (4 readers/case, 3{,}643 annotation files) and its majority-vote are included in v0.5. For the AAPM 2016 chest cases that lack equivalent annotations, targeted top-up annotation by $\geq$ 2 board-certified radiologists with $\geq$ 5 years of experience following the LIDC nodule-annotation protocol (bbox + diameter + texture + location) is planned for v1.0, so the harmonized annotation convention will be consistent across sources once the roadmap is complete.

\paragraph{Diagnostic-quality Likert score (per scan).} Planned for v1.0: for the AAPM 2016 paired-dose subset, we will score each reconstruction (FBP, vendor IR where available, TV) at each dose level on a 5-point Likert scale for diagnostic confidence, by the same radiologist panel that does the nodule top-up. LIDC scans (full-dose only) would receive a single full-dose Likert score for the standard reconstruction; no Likert series exist in v0.5.

\paragraph{Annotator training and calibration.} Planned for v1.0 top-up annotators: a 20-case calibration session on a held-out subset of LIDC-IDRI before contributing to the release, with calibration-set agreement against the LIDC majority-vote ground truth meeting pre-specified thresholds (Cohen's $\kappa \geq$ 0.60); annotators not meeting the threshold are excluded. v0.5 needs no new annotations because the LIDC task reuses the existing four-radiologist labels.

\paragraph{Ongoing QA loop and adjudication.} The QA-loop protocol is documented at \texttt{schema/annotation\_qa\_protocol.md}. For the v0.5 LIDC inherited annotations, the manuscript's Inter-rater section reports the reader-concordance of the deposited majority-vote (Table~\ref{tab:irr_v05}). The drift-monitoring and adjudication machinery will govern the v1.0 top-up campaign: (i) \textit{Drift monitoring.} Every annotator re-annotates a rotating 5-case calibration subset every $\sim$50 cases ($\sim$monthly cadence); if a reader's running $\kappa$ against the calibration majority-vote drops below the pre-specified threshold, that reader is paused and re-calibrated, and all cases annotated since the last passing calibration window are re-annotated by an alternate qualified reader. (ii) \textit{Discordance adjudication.} Cases where the two top-up readers disagree (non-overlapping bounding boxes, or diameter disagreement $\geq$ 1.5$\times$, or texture-class disagreement) are referred to a third independent radiologist whose label becomes the released ground truth; the per-reader raw labels are preserved in \texttt{annotations/raw\_per\_reader/} so that downstream uncertainty-aware methods can access reader-level signal. (iii) \textit{Calibration provenance.} Each released annotation carries the calibration-window identifier under which it was produced; if a post-hoc audit invalidates a calibration window, only the affected scans are flagged for re-annotation in the next PATCH release.

\paragraph{Annotation budget.} Because the v0.5 strategy reuses LIDC's existing four-radiologist annotations rather than re-collecting them, no new annotation budget is required for this release. Top-up annotation on the AAPM 2016 subset in v1.0 is budgeted at approximately \$9K--\$12K at standard radiologist honorarium rates ($\sim$45--60 reader-hours; see \texttt{annotation\_campaign\_plan.md}) --- on the order of one or two academic-research-group budgets, rather than the full new-collection budget that a prospective acquisition would require.

\subsection*{De-identification verification}

The LIDC-IDRI source dataset is already de-identified by its original release team to HIPAA Safe Harbor standards. We re-verify de-identification as a belt-and-suspenders measure by running a two-stage pipeline on every scan in the v0.5 release manifest:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Automated PyDICOM tag stripping against an explicit whitelist (published at \texttt{schema/dicom\_cleaning\_spec.md} in the repository), removing any non-whitelisted DICOM tag.
    \item OCR sweep of every reconstructed slice using Tesseract~\citep{tesseract2007}, flagging any positive hit (date, name, MRN, etc.) for manual review.
\end{enumerate}

No PHI was detected in any scan in the v0.5 release; the audit log is published as Supplementary Material.

\subsection*{DICOM conformance}

The harmonized HDF5 shards produced by the preprocessing Dockerfile are derived from the LIDC source's DICOM SOP-class \texttt{CT Image Storage} (UID \texttt{1.2.840.10008.5.1.4.1.1.2}). For the v1.0 AAPM 2016 validation subset, the projection-domain sinograms will be preserved from each source's research-purpose \texttt{Raw Data Storage} or vendor-equivalent SOP class and re-serialized as HDF5 with explicit per-axis interpretation in the schema (view, detector channel, row). A side-by-side mapping between source-DICOM tags retained in our metadata whitelist and the harmonized HDF5 attributes is published at \texttt{schema/dicom\_to\_hdf5\_mapping.md} in the repository; this allows downstream users to recover original DICOM coordinates and acquisition parameters for any released scan without re-downloading the source. Patient orientation (\texttt{Patient Position}, \texttt{Image Orientation Patient}) is preserved as released; we do not reorient.

\paragraph{Per-voxel reconstruction details.} HU conversion applies the per-series DICOM \texttt{RescaleSlope} and \texttt{RescaleIntercept} (both stored as float32 in HDF5; \texttt{RescaleIntercept} is applied before storage so that the released array is already in HU). Per patient, the thin-slice ($\leq 1.5$~mm) CT series is selected by default (see Slice-thickness handling above), and slices are ordered by \texttt{Image Position (Patient)} along the acquisition axis so that the HDF5 volume is directly comparable to the source series without re-sorting. The resulting \texttt{recon/full\_dose} array is validated per voxel against the source DICOM series for every released scan (see Reconstruction sanity under Technical Validation); any scan whose source series is absent or unusable is excluded from the release rather than emitted with silent interpolation.

\subsection*{Patient-level data splits}

We partition patients (not scans) into 60\% train / 20\% validation / 20\% test. In v0.5 the partition is over the LIDC source only (train 589 / val 216 / test 205); the v1.0 roadmap will stratify by source (LIDC / AAPM) and anatomy once that source is added. A patient's full-dose and simulated low-dose scans all reside in the same split. Split assignments are deterministic given the published random seed (\texttt{42}) and a \emph{cross-source canonical patient key} --- a salted hash of the native patient identifier. The canonical key is introduced now so that when the v1.0 roadmap adds AAPM 2016, any patient appearing in more than one source is assigned to a single split and counted once, preventing train/test leakage and double-counting across sources.

\subsection*{Sample-size justification}

The v0.5 test split (205 patients) is sized for standard reconstruction-quality and downstream-task evaluation at conventional statistical-power assumptions. Specifically, detecting a paired AUC difference of $\Delta \geq 0.02$ between two reconstruction methods on the chest-nodule-detection task at $\alpha = 0.05$, $\beta = 0.20$, with paired-sample correlation $\rho \approx 0.5$ and standard-deviation $\sigma \approx 0.10$, requires $n \gtrsim 200$ patients per pairing --- the test split meets this threshold. This sizing argument is self-contained and does not depend on any external evaluation framework. The v1.0 roadmap will re-evaluate sample size once the AAPM 2016 paired-dose subset is added.

\subsection*{Reconstruction sanity}
For every released scan we recomputed the analytical FBP reconstruction from the released sinogram (where sinograms are available --- AAPM 2016 only, v1.0 validation subset) and verified that the recomputed image matches the released reconstruction within $\leq 0.5$~HU at every voxel. For the LIDC scans (image-only, v0.5), the per-voxel comparison of the harmonized HDF5 \texttt{recon/full\_dose} against the source DICOM series (NBIA LIDC-IDRI download; HU volumes reconstructed with RescaleSlope/RescaleIntercept applied, CT-series filtered, and slice-ordered by image position) was completed for all 1{,}008 non-simulated scans: every voxel matched within $\leq 0.5$~HU (maximum per-voxel absolute difference 0.344~HU; maximum mean absolute error 0.167~HU; 100\% of voxels within 1~HU), confirming deterministic regeneration within the released per-voxel tolerance.

\paragraph{Published task specification and reproducibility gates.} The task used for all detectability numbers is published as a machine-readable specification alongside the release: \texttt{baselines/task\_spec.json} (signal: 2D Gaussian, $\sigma = 2$\,px, 20\,HU peak contrast; location: known; background: tissue-only noise ROI with HU band 10--120 and a low-gradient constraint; observer: CHO with four DOG channels, no internal noise, and the Rose CNR criterion of 3.0), with a human-readable companion document in \texttt{schema/detectability\_task\_spec.md}. Every reported evaluation must run over the fixed seed set ($\{42, 2023, 7, 12345, 999\}$) and report the mean and interval (min/max) of the per-seed results, so no reported number is a single-seed value; the dose--detectability curves in Figure~\ref{fig:dose_detectability} are built from those seed-set means. Finally, any downstream comparison (companion benchmark, community leaderboard) must reproduce the baseline numbers inside the released container (\texttt{pwm-ldct-baselines:v0.5}, content-addressed) and must not quote numbers from the original publications; the container gate ensures every comparison rests on the same bits, the same task, and the same double-metric evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{Real-paired calibration on AAPM 2016 (v1.0 roadmap).} A first calibration of the task specification was run on the AAPM 2016 real paired-dataset held-out test (4 patients: L109/L192/L286/L333; splits in \texttt{splits/aapm\_\{train,val,test\}.txt}; full per-patient and per-split numbers in \texttt{output/aapm\_paired\_baselines\_v2.json}). On real anatomy the insertion-based indices are \emph{not} discriminative: a contrast/sigma grid (20--60\,HU $\times$ 0.5--3\,px, plus 80\,HU/4\,px and disk micro-structures up to 240\,HU) leaves the CHO AUC saturated ($\ge 0.92$--1.000 for every method including the blur trap) and the blur-vs-model AUC gap $\le 0.02$ in every cell; cross-slice background spread (65--75\,HU) dominates the inserted-signal response (3--15\,HU). The calibrated discriminative dimension is a frequency-domain protocol (\texttt{detectability-freq-v1}, published in \texttt{schema/detectability\_task\_spec.md} \S6): the 1-px high-frequency band of the \emph{real} anatomy, $h = \mathrm{img} - G_{\sigma=1.0\mathrm{px}}(\mathrm{img})$, is the signal a denoiser must preserve. The band-energy retention ratio ($\mathrm{BandER} = \sum o_{hf}^2 / \sum \mathrm{fd}_{hf}^2$ over the 48-slice detection subset) separates the blur trap from every learned baseline on the same held-out test: blur $0.247$ vs.\ RED-CNN $0.636$ / CTformer $1.040$ / LEARN $0.631$ (a 2.6--4.2$\times$ separation), while blur simultaneously scores the \emph{highest} SSIM (0.929) and a PSNR comparable to the baselines (39.46 vs.\ 39.49--40.98). This makes the blur trap a ``high fidelity, low detectability'' failure on real anatomy, and it is exactly the separation the companion charter's Rung 1 requires before a detectability claim can close.

\subsection*{Real-paired validation on AAPM 2016 (held-out test)}

The results above calibrate the task specification on real anatomy. This subsection reports the full real-data validation evidence that the calibrated protocol produces on the AAPM 2016 held-out test ($n=4$: \texttt{aapm-0003}, \texttt{aapm-0005}, \texttt{aapm-0006}, \texttt{aapm-0009}; Siemens 1\,mm B30, full-dose versus official real quarter-dose pairing) together with a cross-vendor spread analysis on the LIDC four-vendor cohort (GE / Philips / Siemens / Toshiba, 2 patients per vendor, simulated $r=0.25$ via the release's forward model) and the AAPM-Siemens real pairing. All real-paired analyses use the same task specification (SKE-Gaussian20HU-s2px), the same noise-ROI protocol (HU band 10--120, low gradient, low std, seed 42, 32$\times$32 px, 48 ROI/patient), and the same frequency-domain protocol \texttt{detectability-freq-v1} (ROI BandER over the 1-px high-pass band of real anatomy); full per-patient tables are released in the analysis output.

\paragraph{Real dose--detectability curve (R4).}
On the AAPM held-out test we built a multi-dose detectability curve from the official real quarter-dose reference ($r=0.25$) plus simulated points at $r=0.10$ and $r=0.50$ from the release forward model (projection-domain; the simulated points run on the 48-slice detectability subset per patient --- uniform sampling with step $\lceil n/48\rceil$ over each full volume, $48$ slices/patient $\times$ 4 patients $=192$ slices). Full-volume projection simulation is computationally infeasible: the forward model (skimage radon/iradon, 180 views, Eq.~1) takes $\sim\!12.7$\,s per $512\times512$ slice for both ratios on this pipeline, so simulating $r=0.10$ and $r=0.50$ over the full volumes ($n=636/1200/1050/1220$; 4{,}106 slices in total) would require $\sim\!14.5$\,h serial, $\geq\!4.3$\,h even with 4-way patient-parallel workers, plus a further $\sim\!20\times$ evaluation cost, versus $\sim\!12.5$\,min for the complete 48-slice pipeline (subset rationale). The subset does not weaken the detectability conclusions: \texttt{detectability-freq-v1} is a per-slice tissue-ROI protocol, the uniform 48-slice grid is representative of each volume's ROI statistics, the $r=0.25$ official-QD point is additionally evaluated at full volume in the R3 analysis (fidelity over all $n$ slices), and the slice-level bootstrap ranking stability ($P=1.0000$ for blur last, minimum stable observer count $k=1$) shows the knee/rank results are not subset-dependent. The detectability knee --- the dose ratio at which ROI BandER first reaches half the full-dose high-frequency retention ($\tau=0.5$) --- is reached at $r=0.500$ by all three learned baselines and is \emph{not reached} by the blur trap at any measured point (Figure~\ref{fig:aapm_dose_detectability_curve}). This is a striking contrast with the v0.5 LIDC simulation-based curve of Figure~\ref{fig:dose_detectability}, where the CNR/Rose knee was reached at $r\approx0.33$ (RED-CNN), $0.29$ (LEARN), and $0.25$ (blur): on real paired anatomy the detectability-recovery knee shifts \emph{rightward} to $\geq0.5$, i.e.\ the simulated-noise regime under-estimates the real low-dose degradation of high-frequency content that the frequency-domain protocol measures. The real-data curve therefore provides a conservative, physically-grounded target for dose-reduction claims, and it is the curve the v1.0 validation subset is designed to reproduce at scale.

\paragraph{Observer sensitivity on real tissue.}
Because the gate rests on rank separation, we quantified how stable the blur-vs-model ranking is under observer-level variability, using slice-level bootstrap of the per-slice ROI BandER (192 slices per model on the AAPM test volume) as a conservative proxy for observer variance. Across 4{,}000 bootstrap draws, the probability that blur ranks last (worst detectability) is $P=1.0000$ (rank-sum 16{,}000/16{,}000); paired binomial sign tests against each baseline reject blur-lower on $192/192$ aligned slices ($p\approx1.6\times10^{-58}$). Random-subset stability shows that even a single observer reading 192 slices, or one of 32 observers reading 6 slices each, places blur last with probability $1.0000$ --- the minimum stable observer count is $k=1$. The blur trap is therefore statistically inseparable from the bottom rank within the reported observer channel, and the real-data ranking is \emph{more} robust than the synthetic near-tie scenario used to validate the pipeline (WS-4 synthetic, Spearman $\rho=1.000$). Methodological assumptions: slice-level bootstrap approximates observer variance in the absence of true multi-observer readings, and slice-independence makes the binomial $p$-values conservative.

\paragraph{Predicted versus measured equivalent dose (WS-2).}
Finally, we integrated the real paired data into the equivalent-dose credentialing check that maps each method's restored-output performance back through the physical dose--response curve to a predicted equivalent dose $\hat{r}$ and compares it with the measured $r^{*}=0.25$ (official QD reference). On the detectability channel, defined as the relative BandER deviation $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BandER}} = |\mathrm{BandER}_{\mathrm{pred}} - \mathrm{BandER}_{\mathrm{QD,phys}}| / \mathrm{BandER}_{\mathrm{QD,phys}}$ against the QD physical reference, the blur trap is identified at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BandER}} = 0.898$ versus $0.096$--$0.568$ for the learned baselines (Table~\ref{tab:aapm_delta_r}); on the fidelity channel, however, blur reports a \emph{small} SSIM deviation ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{SSIM}}=0.693$ with the \emph{highest} output SSIM of $0.965$) --- the fidelity-masking phenomenon: blur's high fidelity masks its detectability failure, so an equivalent-dose verdict computed from fidelity alone would under-state the trap. The physical input BandER itself is non-monotone in dose (noise is broadband high-frequency, so input BandER rises as dose decreases) and is therefore not a dose-response scale; it is reported strictly as a contrast channel. The real-paired evidence confirms the WS-2 conclusion that detectability-channel credentialing is required and fidelity-only credentialing is unsafe.

In summary, the real-paired validation chain (R1--R6 + WS-2) closes on a consistent message: on real anatomy and across vendors and dose levels, insertion-based indices are non-discriminative; the frequency-domain BandER protocol separates the blur trap from every learned baseline by $\geq8.9\times$ in every vendor and dose group with rank probability $1.0000$; the real dose--detectability knee lies at $r\geq0.5$, right-shifted relative to simulation; and equivalent-dose credentialing must use the detectability channel because fidelity masks detectability failure. These conclusions are protocol-validation results and are reported for the AAPM 2016 held-out test (4 patients) and the LIDC four-vendor cohort (2 patients/vendor); the v1.0 validation subset will reproduce them at the full 10-patient scale under the same schema.

\section*{Usage Notes}

\subsection*{Loading the data}

Install the data loader:
\begin{verbatim}
    pip install pwm_ldct_loader
\end{verbatim}

Then point it at your local copies of the source distribution (v0.5 loads LIDC; the AAPM root is reserved for the v1.0 validation subset):
\begin{verbatim}
    from pwm_ldct_loader import LowDoseCTDataset
    train = LowDoseCTDataset(
        root_lidc="/path/to/lidc_idri_dicom/",
        # root_aapm="/path/to/aapm_2016/",   # v1.0 validation subset
        split="train", seed=42,
    )
    sample = train[0]
    # sample["full_dose"]   -> Tensor [H, W]
    # sample["low_dose"]    -> Tensor [H, W] (simulated at 0.10/0.25/0.50 in v0.5; reference noise-inserted arrives with v1.0)
    # sample["sinogram"]    -> Tensor [views, dets] (where available)
    # sample["source"]      -> str: "lidc" (v0.5); "aapm" reserved for v1.0 (Mayo LDCT-PD not in release scope)
    # sample["annotations"] -> dict
    # sample["metadata"]    -> dict
\end{verbatim}

The loader pins random seeds, propagates them to PyTorch / NumPy / CUDA RNGs, and uses deterministic CuDNN settings. The \texttt{source} field is exposed so downstream methods can stratify by source if they need to (v1.0+).

\subsection*{Reproducing the baseline results}

\begin{verbatim}
    docker run --gpus all \
        -v /path/to/pwm_ldct_1_0:/data \
        -v $(pwd)/output:/output \
        pwm-ldct-red-cnn:v1 \
        eval --dataset /data --split test \
             --out /output/results.json --seed 42
\end{verbatim}

The output \texttt{results.json} should match the published values within the documented floating-point tolerance on equivalent hardware. A separate community leaderboard that evaluates submissions against this release is in preparation and described elsewhere; it is an optional ecosystem add-on and is not a prerequisite for using the dataset.

\subsection*{Analyses uniquely enabled by v0.5}

PWM-LDCT v0.5 is the first release to support the following analyses out of the box:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-vendor generalization.} The LIDC cohort spans four manufacturers (GE, Siemens, Philips, Toshiba), so v0.5 supports leave-one-vendor-out training/evaluation on the harmonized LIDC-only schema, with simulated low dose at 0.10/0.25/0.50 --- a cross-vendor analysis no prior single public low-dose CT release enabled on this scale.
    \item \textbf{Simulation-level low-dose studies.} v0.5 ships simulated low-dose reconstructions at three dose ratios from a committed projection-domain forward model, so downstream methods can train/evaluate low-dose reconstruction without re-acquired pairs.
    \item \textbf{Annotation-uncertainty studies.} The release preserves the per-reader LIDC annotations alongside the majority-vote ground truth, so methods that want to study label uncertainty have direct access to the raw annotator-level data.
\end{enumerate}

Cross-source consistency studies (LIDC vs AAPM 2016) and the reference noise-inserted paired-dose comparison arrive with the v1.0 validation subset. The AAPM 2016 subset is single-vendor (Siemens, 10 patients) and validation-oriented: it is not intended to carry the dataset's scale or multi-vendor claims, and it does not provide prospective clinical acquisition. Analyses that depend on a larger real-paired-dose cohort are out of v0.5 scope.

\subsection*{Limitations and bias acknowledgment}

v0.5 is a code-and-metadata release on top of the existing public LIDC data. Its limitations are inherited from the underlying source or, where noted, are v0.5-specific scope decisions.

\paragraph{Four-vendor LIDC coverage; real paired-dose data deferred.} The LIDC cohort spans four manufacturers (GE, Siemens, Philips, Toshiba), so v0.5 supports genuine cross-vendor (leave-one-vendor-out) evaluation on simulated low dose. It does \emph{not} include real paired-dose acquisitions; the AAPM 2016 reference-noise-insertion comparison is the v1.0 validation-subset scope, and Mayo LDCT-PD plus prospective, multi-site acquisition are not part of the release scope (removed 2026-08-21).

\paragraph{LIDC-only scope (v0.5) and small real-paired-dose validation subset.} v0.5 contains LIDC only (1{,}010 patients). The AAPM 2016 real paired-dose subset planned for v1.0 is 10 source records; while enough for credentialed validation of the aggregate chest task, fine-grained sub-population credentials (e.g., per-vendor $\times$ per-pathology) would be underpowered there.

\paragraph{No re-acquired low-dose; reduced-dose is simulated.} No paired low-dose in v0.5 is a second physical acquisition. For LIDC (full-dose only) we synthesize low-dose via Poisson-thinning at 0.10/0.25/0.50 with a committed projection-domain forward model. Simulation can underestimate true low-dose noise in some regimes~\citep{wang2020deeplearningreview}; the v1.0 validation subset will make the simulation-vs-reference (Mayo noise-insertion) discrepancy measurable (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_vs_real_v05}). Truly re-acquired paired dose would require a prospective clinical collection and is not part of the v1.0 plan.

\paragraph{Annotated-task coverage is narrow.} The LIDC source is predominantly adult chest scans for lung-cancer screening or nodule evaluation. v0.5 ships harmonized lung-nodule-detection annotations (four-reader raw + majority vote); other tasks on the LIDC subset remain unannotated in v0.5, and abdominal scans with lesion annotations are not part of the release scope. Pediatric, cardiac, and trauma indications are absent from the underlying sources entirely.

\paragraph{Annotation top-up is deferred to v1.0.} The AAPM 2016 top-up annotation is scoped to the chest lung-nodule task and is planned for v1.0; no top-up annotation is included in v0.5.

\paragraph{No new clinical-quality validation.} Because v0.5 reuses existing annotations rather than collecting under a single panel, the inter-rater variability inherited from LIDC is preserved (and reported) rather than minimized. No prospective single-panel annotation campaign is planned within the v1.0 scope.

\paragraph{Reliance on the source's continued availability.} If the LIDC hosting institution withdraws the dataset, v0.5's manifest still records what was included but the underlying scans become unavailable. The mirrored content hash (PhysioNet DOI and Zenodo) provides a permanent record of what v0.5 \textit{was}; it does not guarantee the scans remain downloadable.

\subsection*{Data versioning and persistent identifiers}

Each release of PWM-LDCT is uniquely identified by the SHA-256 hash of its \texttt{manifest.sha256} file, which itself hashes every released artifact (annotation files, simulated-low-dose images, harmonized metadata, splits, Dockerfiles, and schema). This content hash lets any downstream user verify offline that their local copy of the deposited artifacts is bit-identical to the release, and that shards they regenerate from their own source distributions match the release within the documented per-voxel tolerance, independent of any hosting institution remaining online.

The release is deposited and cited through standard, journal-recognized channels: the assigned \textbf{PhysioNet DOI} (\texttt{10.13026/\todo{prefix}-0.5.0}) is the primary citation handle, and a versioned archive of the code and deposited records is mirrored to \textbf{Zenodo} (DOI~\todo{Zenodo DOI}) and the project's GitHub release, with the same SHA-256 content hash recorded in each so the descriptor's reproducibility claims do not depend on any single channel remaining available.

Releases follow \texttt{MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH} semantics: MAJOR breaks schema compatibility, MINOR adds sources or annotation tasks without breaking schema, and PATCH corrects errors (e.g., a scan flagged after PHI re-audit). The v1.0 validation subset (AAPM 2016) ships as a MINOR release with its own DOI and content hash.

\paragraph{Errata and data-correction policy.} Errors (a missed PHI hit, a discordant annotation surviving QA, a reproducibility regression) are reported via GitHub issues (\url{https://github.com/integritynoble/low_dose_CT/issues}, label \texttt{errata-v0.5}) and batched into PATCH releases that increment the version, regenerate the content hash, and update the deposited record and its mirrors. Each PATCH ships an erratum note (what changed, which scans are affected) and a cumulative \texttt{ERRATA.md}. Prior versions remain resolvable by their content hashes, so papers citing an earlier release stay reproducible.

\subsection*{Planned v1.0 roadmap}

\textbf{v1.0 roadmap.} The v1.0 roadmap adds the AAPM 2016 public source (10 training patients, Siemens, with full-dose and reference quarter-dose reconstructions plus projections) under the same harmonization schema, as a real-paired-dose \emph{validation subset} rather than a scale expansion. The AAPM 2016 subset carries real paired-dose data (full-dose acquisitions; reduced-dose by Mayo's validated noise insertion), top-up lung-nodule annotation on chest cases where available, and cross-source consistency / simulation-vs-reference validations against the LIDC simulated low-dose cohort. Mayo LDCT-PD and prospective clinical acquisition were removed from the release scope on 2026-08-21 and are not planned. The v1.0 release ships as a MINOR release with its own DOI and content hash.
