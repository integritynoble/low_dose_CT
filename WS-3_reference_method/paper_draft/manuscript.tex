\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Low-dose CT reconstruction research is rich in methods but poor in
\emph{shared, reusable derived data}: published methods release a model
or a number, rarely the per-case reconstructed volumes, the per-pixel
uncertainty that a clinical user would triage on, or a machine-checkable
certificate of diagnostic equivalence. We describe a deposited corpus
that supplies exactly this layer on top of the multi-vendor PWM-LDCT
benchmark~\citep{ws1dataset}. For every test scan, at four dose levels
($10\%$, $25\%$, $50\%$, $100\%$) and across multiple CT vendors, the
corpus provides: (i) a \textbf{reference reconstruction} produced by an
open, fully specified unrolled-iterative pipeline; (ii) a
\textbf{per-pixel uncertainty map} from a five-model deep ensemble; (iii)
the \textbf{paired per-pixel error map} against the full-dose reference,
enabling calibration studies without re-running any model; (iv)
\textbf{downstream-task output maps} (lung-nodule detection scores); (v)
machine-readable \textbf{signal-equivalence credentials} computed under
the framework of \citet{ws2framework}; and (vi) the matched
reconstructions of five baseline methods for comparison. Every record is
content-addressed, released under CC~BY~4.0, and accompanied by an
Apache-2.0 generating pipeline packaged as a pinned Docker RunBundle so
the corpus is regenerable bit-for-bit within floating-point tolerance.
Technical validation reports reconstruction fidelity, cross-vendor
consistency, and---centrally for reuse---the agreement between the
released uncertainty maps and the released error maps (Spearman $\rho$).
The corpus is intended for reuse in uncertainty-calibration research,
cross-vendor robustness studies, downstream-task evaluation under dose
reduction, and as a stable comparison baseline for new reconstruction
methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background \& Summary}
\label{sec:background}

Deep-learning low-dose CT reconstruction has produced a decade of method
papers reporting fractional-dB gains~\citep{chen2017redcnn,wang2020deeplearningreview,song2022scorebased},
yet the field shares almost no \emph{derived} data. Two structural gaps
follow. First, reconstructed volumes are seldom released: a researcher
who wants to study how reconstruction error distributes across anatomy,
dose, or vendor must retrain each method from scratch. Second, the two
quantities most relevant to clinical deployment---\emph{per-pixel
uncertainty} and a \emph{certificate of task-level equivalence}---are
almost never published as data a third party can inspect. A deployer
choosing a reconstruction for a new scanner, or a calibration researcher
studying whether predicted variance tracks true error, has nothing
reusable to build on.

The raw substrate for closing these gaps now exists: the PWM-LDCT
benchmark~\citep{ws1dataset} provides multi-vendor paired (full-dose,
low-dose) CT, extending the field's two single-vendor real-paired
resources, AAPM-2016~\citep{aapm2016challenge} and Mayo
LDCT-PD~\citep{mccollough2020mayo}, both Siemens-only. What has been
missing is the \emph{derived layer}: a uniformly produced set of
reconstructions, uncertainty maps, error maps, task outputs, and
equivalence credentials over that substrate.

This Data Descriptor releases that layer. We generate every record with a
single, fully specified open pipeline---an unrolled iterative
reconstruction with a weight-shared denoiser and a five-model deep
ensemble for uncertainty (Methods)---and deposit the outputs as a
content-addressed corpus. We emphasise that the pipeline is a
\emph{means of generating reproducible data}, not the object of this
paper; its architecture is reported in full only so the data are
reproducible and the uncertainty maps are interpretable.

\paragraph{Relationship to the PWM-LDCT benchmark (WS-1).} This corpus is
deliberately disjoint from the underlying benchmark dataset. WS-1
releases the \emph{raw paired CT scans}; the present corpus releases the
\emph{reconstruction, uncertainty, error, task-output, and credential
records derived from them}. The two are intended to be cited together:
WS-1 as the input data, this corpus as the reference derived data.

\paragraph{Reuse value.} The corpus enables, without any model retraining:
(1) \textbf{uncertainty-calibration research}---the paired
(uncertainty map, error map) records are a ready-made benchmark for
calibration metrics and recalibration methods; (2) \textbf{cross-vendor
robustness studies}---reconstructions and errors are vendor-labelled,
supporting leave-one-vendor-out analyses; (3) \textbf{downstream-task
evaluation under dose reduction}---released task-score maps let others
study how dose affects detection without re-deriving reconstructions; (4)
\textbf{credential-based comparison}---the machine-readable
signal-equivalence credentials are a stable target that new methods can
report against under a content-addressed framework hash; and (5) a
\textbf{stable comparison baseline}---five matched baseline
reconstructions accompany the reference so new methods compare against
identical inputs.

\paragraph{Summary of what is released.} \autoref{tab:records_overview}
summarises the record types; \autoref{sec:datarecords} gives the full
file-level manifest. All records, for all scans, at all dose levels and
vendors, are deposited together under a single repository DOI
(\datadoi).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

This section specifies how each released record was generated. It is
included to make the corpus reproducible and the uncertainty maps
interpretable; it is not a methodological contribution of this Data
Descriptor.

\subsection{Input data and provenance}
Inputs are the PWM-LDCT~\citep{ws1dataset} scans at signal ratios $r \in
\{0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$, patient-level split into train/validation/test,
vendor-labelled. Reference (target) images are the patient's full-dose
reconstruction. No scan crosses the train/test boundary at the patient
level; all released records are for the held-out test split.

\paragraph{Low-dose provenance (real-paired vs simulated).} Each
(vendor, dose) stratum is one of two kinds, recorded per record in
\texttt{scan\_meta.json} (\texttt{low\_dose\_origin} $\in$
\{\texttt{real\_paired}, \texttt{simulated}\}): \emph{real-paired}
acquisitions are genuine repeat low-dose scans from the source
benchmark~\citep{ws1dataset}; \emph{simulated} low-dose is generated from
the full-dose projections by Poisson photon-count reduction in the
\texttt{ct\_radon} forward model~\citep{pwmcore} at the stated $r$. Reusers
must not treat the two as interchangeable: simulated strata characterise
algorithmic behaviour under an idealised noise model, real-paired strata
characterise behaviour under true acquisition physics. The per-stratum
breakdown is reported in \autoref{tab:cohort} and carried in every
record's metadata.

\paragraph{The $r = 1.00$ level.} Full dose is the reference, not a
credentialed dose level. Where an $r = 1.00$ reconstruction is released it
is the reference image $x^{\textrm{ref}}$ itself; no error map is emitted
at $r = 1.00$ (the error against the reference is identically zero) and no
credential is defined there (signal-equivalence credentials compare a
\emph{reduced}-dose method against the full-dose reference). All released
error maps, task-score maps, and credentials are therefore at $r \in
\{0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$.

\paragraph{Ethics and human-subjects provenance.} This corpus contains no
new human-subjects data: every record is a derived image or a numerical
summary computed from the PWM-LDCT benchmark~\citep{ws1dataset}, which
governs the underlying acquisitions, their institutional-review-board
approvals, and informed-consent basis. The specific governing approvals ---
\todo{name the WS-1 IRB protocol number(s) and the Mayo LDCT-PD / AAPM~2016
data-use agreement reference} --- are stated in the WS-1 source descriptor and
restated here at submission. The released NIfTI volumes carry no
protected health information: voxel arrays only, with all DICOM
patient-identifying header fields removed at generation, and
\texttt{scan\_id} is the WS-1 pseudonymous identifier (never an
institutional medical-record number). Redistribution follows the
per-source basis of the WS-1 release, set out under
\emph{Licensing and redistribution} (\autoref{sec:datarecords}); reusers
remain bound by the source data-use terms, in particular for any attempt
to re-link records to source acquisitions. No date-of-service, geographic,
or demographic field finer than the anatomy/vendor labels in
\texttt{scan\_meta.json} is released.

\subsection{Reconstruction pipeline}
\label{sec:method_overview}
Reconstructions are produced by an unrolled iterative
scheme~\citep{adler2018learned,mardani2018neural}. Let $\mathcal{R}:
\R^{H \times W} \to \R^{n_{\textrm{views}} \times n_{\textrm{dets}}}$ be
the Radon forward operator and $\mathcal{R}^\dagger$ its filtered
back-projection adjoint, both taken from the open
\texttt{pwm\_core.contrib.modalities.ct\_radon}
implementation~\citep{pwmcore} validated against the PWM modality test
suite; we do not re-implement them, so any user regenerating the corpus
inherits the identical, version-pinned forward model.

Given a low-dose sinogram $y$, the reconstruction $\hat{x}$ is computed by
$K=10$ data-consistency / denoising iterations
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:unrolled}). The denoiser $f_\theta$ is a U-Net
($\approx 4$\,M parameters, four encoder / four decoder stages with
$(32,64,128,256)$ channels, GELU, GroupNorm) whose weights are shared
across iterations; the step size $\tau$ is a learned scalar initialised at
$0.5/\|\mathcal{R}\|_2^2$.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
    \caption{Unrolled iterative reconstruction (per ensemble member)}
    \label{alg:unrolled}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \State $x^{(0)} \gets \textrm{TV-FBP}(y)$ \Comment{TV-regularized warm start}
        \For{$k = 0, 1, \ldots, K{-}1$}
            \State $x^{(k+\sfrac{1}{2})} \gets x^{(k)} - \tau \cdot \mathcal{R}^\dagger \!\left( \mathcal{R}(x^{(k)}) - y \right)$ \Comment{Data consistency}
            \State $x^{(k+1)} \gets f_\theta\!\left( x^{(k+\sfrac{1}{2})} \right)$ \Comment{Learned denoising}
        \EndFor
        \State \Return $\hat{x} \gets x^{(K)}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{sec:uq}
Each released reconstruction is the mean of a five-model deep
ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017deep}: five instances of the
architecture above, identical except for the random seed governing weight
initialisation and data ordering (seeds $\{42,43,44,45,46\}$). For a
low-dose input $y$, member $m$ produces $\hat{x}_m(y)$; the released point
estimate and per-pixel uncertainty are
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}(y) = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{m=1}^{5} \hat{x}_m(y), \qquad
    \sigma(y)_{ij} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{5}\sum_{m=1}^{5}\left(\hat{x}_m(y)_{ij} - \hat{x}(y)_{ij}\right)^2}.
\end{equation}
Deep ensembles are chosen over MC dropout because they calibrate better
under distribution shift~\citep{ovadia2019uncertainty,gal2016dropout} and
have bounded ($5\times$) inference cost. The released $\sigma(y)$ map is
the quantity intended for case-level triage; its \emph{validity} as a
triage signal is established in Technical Validation by comparison with
the released error map.

\subsection{Derived per-record quantities}
For each test scan and dose level the pipeline emits, in addition to
$\hat{x}(y)$ and $\sigma(y)$: the \textbf{per-pixel error map}
$e(y)_{ij} = |\hat{x}(y)_{ij} - x^{\textrm{ref}}_{ij}|$ against the
full-dose reference, and a \textbf{lung-nodule detection score map} from a
frozen pre-trained detector applied to $\hat{x}(y)$
(detector specified in Section~\ref{sec:detector}). (Version~1 of the
corpus releases this single chest / lung-nodule task; additional tasks
such as abdominal liver-lesion segmentation are deferred to a future
release so that the released records and the task description remain in
exact correspondence.) In addition, at the \emph{cohort} level---one record per $(\textrm{task}, r,
\textrm{subpopulation}, \textrm{method})$ stratum rather than per
scan---the pipeline emits a \textbf{signal-equivalence credential}
(5-tuple $(r, T, \varepsilon, \alpha, \Pi)$ with verdict and confidence
interval) computed with the \texttt{pwm\_dose\_equivalence}
library~\citep{ws2framework}; each credential is internally audited with
\texttt{pwm-audit} before it is written, and is regenerable from the
released task-score arrays. The five baseline methods
(Section~\ref{sec:datarecords}) are run through the identical emit path so
their records are field-for-field comparable.

\subsection{Frozen downstream-task detector}
\label{sec:detector}
The lung-nodule detection score maps are produced by a single
\emph{frozen} detector held fixed across every method, dose level, and
record, so the task layer measures the diagnostic content each
reconstruction preserves rather than any adaptation of the detector
itself. The detector is \textbf{nnDetection}~\citep{baumgartner2021nndetection}---a
self-configuring 3D Retina~U-Net---run in its published configuration
without modification; the exact release is pinned in Supplementary
Table~S2. It is trained \emph{once} on the \textbf{LUNA16}
benchmark~\citep{setio2017luna16}, the 888-scan
LIDC/IDRI~\citep{lidc_idri} subset annotated for pulmonary nodules with
$\geq 3/4$ radiologist agreement, and is never fine-tuned on PWM-LDCT
reconstructions. The LUNA16 training patients are disjoint from the
PWM-LDCT cohort, so the detector is an external, leakage-free evaluator
applied identically to all methods.

\paragraph{Operating-point provenance.} The credentialing operating
point is fixed \emph{before} any PWM-LDCT reconstruction is scored: on
the held-out LUNA16 validation fold we take the detection-score
threshold whose free-response ROC (FROC) yields a mean of $0.1$
false-positive detections per scan ($\textrm{FPR}=0.1$) and freeze it
for all subsequent scoring. The released task-score maps are the raw,
pre-threshold detector scores, so reusers may re-derive performance at
any operating point; the AUC in \autoref{tab:task_auc} and the task
credentials are read at this single frozen $\textrm{FPR}=0.1$ point,
identical across all methods and doses. The frozen weights, inference
configuration, and version pin are deposited in the RunBundle
(Section~\ref{sec:codeavail}) so the task layer is independently
reproducible.

\subsection{Training and generation configuration}
Models are trained on the PWM-LDCT training split with paired targets at
$r \in \{0.10,0.25,0.50\}$ mixed per minibatch with weights $(0.4,0.4,0.2)$;
augmentation is physical-consistency-preserving only (random flip
$p{=}0.5$; rotation $[-5^\circ,5^\circ]$; window-level shift
$[-50,50]$\,HU). Optimiser AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2018adamw}, cosine LR
with $5\%$ warmup, batch size $4$ at $512\times512$, single A100; CuDNN
deterministic, all RNGs seed-propagated. The complete hyperparameter
table, library version pins, and compute disclosure accompany the deposit
(Supplementary Tables~S1--S3) so the corpus is regenerable.

\section{Data Records}
\label{sec:datarecords}
\label{sec:data_records}

Records are deposited at \datadoi{}, with integrity verified by the
per-file SHA-256 \texttt{MANIFEST.sha256} at the archive root (an optional
content-addressed mirror is noted in Section~\ref{sec:codeavail}); their
licensing follows the per-source basis set out in \emph{Licensing and
redistribution} below. The deposit is a single archive with the directory
layout below; every leaf file is additionally listed with its SHA-256 in
\texttt{MANIFEST.sha256} at the archive root.

\paragraph{Licensing and redistribution.} The corpus inherits the
per-source redistribution basis of the WS-1 benchmark~\citep{ws1dataset},
which redistributes value-added derivatives only where the source licence
permits and otherwise has reusers regenerate them locally. Accordingly:
(i)~all \emph{numerical} records---credentials, \texttt{scan\_meta.json},
\texttt{dataset\_metadata.json}, and the integrity manifest---are released
under CC~BY~4.0, being aggregate statistics and metadata rather than image
derivatives and carrying no protected health information; (ii)~\emph{pixel}
records (reconstruction, uncertainty, error, and task-score maps) derived
from LIDC-IDRI scans are released under CC~BY~3.0, matching the licence
under which WS-1 already redistributes its LIDC-derived simulated low-dose
images; and (iii)~pixel records derived from the AAPM~2016 Low-Dose CT
Grand Challenge and Mayo LDCT-PD scans are \emph{not} redistributed---those
source data-use agreements restrict redistribution of pixel
derivatives---and are instead regenerated locally by reusers from their own
authorised copies of the source data using the deposited Apache-2.0
RunBundle (\autoref{sec:codeavail}), which reproduces them within
floating-point tolerance. The released cross-vendor \emph{credentials} and
fidelity statistics (\autoref{tab:cross_vendor}) are unaffected by (iii),
as they are aggregate numbers rather than redistributed pixels. Generating
code is Apache~2.0.

\paragraph{Directory layout.}
\begin{verbatim}
pwm-ldct-recon-corpus/
  MANIFEST.sha256                 # SHA-256 of every leaf file
  dataset_metadata.json           # ISA-style top-level metadata, DOI, licence
  reconstructions/
    <vendor>/<scan_id>/r<dd>/      # dd in {010,025,050,100}
      recon_mean.nii.gz           # reference reconstruction (this method)
      uncertainty_sigma.nii.gz    # per-pixel ensemble std dev
      error_abs.nii.gz            # |recon - full_dose_ref|
      task_nodule_score.nii.gz    # frozen-detector score map
      scan_meta.json              # vendor, r, anatomy, split, WS-1 source id
  baselines/
    <method>/<vendor>/<scan_id>/r<dd>/  # method in {fbp,redcnn,wgan_vgg,
      recon.nii.gz                      #            transformer,diffusion}
      error_abs.nii.gz
      task_nodule_score.nii.gz
  credentials/                    # cohort-level: one credential per
    <task>/r<dd>/<method>__<vendor>.json  #   (task x dose x method x vendor)
    all_credentials.jsonl         # flat queryable index of every credential
  checksums_and_versions.json     # library version pins for regeneration
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Naming and indexing.} \texttt{scan\_id} is the WS-1 source
identifier, so any record back-links to its raw input. \texttt{r<dd>}
encodes the dose ratio. The flat \texttt{all\_credentials.jsonl} index
lets a reuser query credentials (by vendor, dose, task, verdict) without
walking the tree.

\paragraph{File-format conventions.} All image records are single-file
gzip-compressed NIfTI-1 (\texttt{.nii.gz}), \texttt{float32}, with an
\texttt{sform} set to a scaled identity and voxel spacing carried in the
header \texttt{pixdim}. The affine is an orientation-normalised scaled
identity by design (patient-position and origin fields from the source
DICOM are not propagated, as part of de-identification); records are
therefore in a canonical voxel frame, not the original scanner frame, and
should not be re-registered to source-patient coordinates. Reconstruction,
reference, and error volumes are in
Hounsfield units (HU); uncertainty maps are in HU (the ensemble standard
deviation of the reconstruction); task-score maps are unitless detector
scores in $[0, 1]$. All maps for one record share an identical voxel grid,
so $\hat{x}$, $\sigma$, and $e$ are voxel-aligned and can be stacked
without resampling. JSON records are UTF-8; credentials validate against
the machine-readable schema bundled with the deposit
(\texttt{credential\_schema.json}) and the WS-2 library~\citep{ws2framework}.

\paragraph{Data dictionary.} Each \texttt{scan\_meta.json} carries the
fields in \autoref{tab:scan_meta_fields}; each \texttt{credential.json} is
the WS-2 5-tuple wire format (top-level \texttt{schema\_version},
\texttt{framework\_hash}, and a \texttt{credential} object holding
\texttt{signal\_ratio}, \texttt{task}, \texttt{epsilon}, \texttt{alpha},
\texttt{subpopulation}, \texttt{estimator}, \texttt{n\_test},
\texttt{delta\_mean}, \texttt{delta\_ci\_low}, \texttt{delta\_ci\_high},
\texttt{verdict}, and a \texttt{sample\_size\_check} block), specified
field-by-field in the companion credential reading guide and the bundled
JSON Schema.

\subsection{Baseline reconstructions included for comparison}
\label{sec:baselines}
To make the corpus a self-contained comparison resource, the matched
reconstructions of five baselines are included, each run through the
identical emit path: FBP; RED-CNN~\citep{chen2017redcnn};
WGAN-VGG~\citep{yang2018ctgan}; a transformer
reconstruction~\citep{wang2023ctformer}; and a diffusion / score-based
reconstruction~\citep{song2022scorebased}. Each baseline is reproduced
within $\leq 0.5$\,dB PSNR of its published numbers before its records are
emitted; reproduction recipes accompany the deposit. The baselines are
single-model methods and therefore carry no \texttt{uncertainty\_sigma}
record (ensemble disagreement is defined only for the deep-ensemble
reference method); their record set is reconstruction, error map,
task-score map, and credential.

\section{Technical Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Validation establishes that the released records are fit for reuse. It is
organised around the three properties a reuser depends on: that the
reconstructions are faithful, that they are consistent across vendors, and
that the released uncertainty maps actually track the released error
maps.

\paragraph{Record integrity (construction check).} As a reuse trust
anchor we verify that every released \texttt{error\_abs} map equals its
defining quantity $|\hat{x}(y)-x^{\textrm{ref}}|$ by reconstruction
rather than by assertion. The deposited tool
\texttt{package\_corpus.py\ verify-error-maps} draws a random sample of
records, reloads the reconstruction together with the scan's full-dose
reference (the released $r=1.00$ \texttt{recon\_mean}), recomputes
$|\hat{x}-x^{\textrm{ref}}|$, and confirms it matches the released
\texttt{error\_abs} voxelwise within float32 tolerance
($\leq 10^{-3}$\,HU). The synthetic end-to-end fixture runs this check in
continuous integration on every commit, and a reuser can re-run it on the
deposit with a single command, so the error layer is independently
checkable, not merely trusted.

\paragraph{Reconstruction fidelity.} \autoref{tab:reconstruction_quality}
reports PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS~\citep{zhang2018lpips} of every released
reconstruction against its full-dose reference, per dose level, for the
reference method and the five baselines. These quantify how close each
released volume is to ground truth; they are descriptive data-quality
evidence, not a competition.

\paragraph{Cross-vendor consistency: interpolation vs.\ extrapolation.}
Because every record is vendor-labelled, reusers can study cross-vendor
generalisation directly --- but only if the corpus states which vendors
are \emph{interpolative} (comfortably inside the multi-vendor training
distribution) and which are \emph{extrapolative} (at its edge). We make
this operational with leave-one-vendor-out (LOVO) generation. The
released reference reconstructions are produced by the deployed ensemble
trained on \emph{all} vendors, so for the deposited corpus every vendor
is in-distribution; \autoref{tab:cross_vendor} additionally reconstructs
each vendor's scans with an ensemble trained \emph{without} that vendor
and reports the fidelity drop $\Delta = \mathrm{PSNR}_{\text{in-dist}} -
\mathrm{PSNR}_{\text{held-out}}$. $\Delta$ is the empirical distance of a
vendor from the rest: a small $\Delta$ marks an \textbf{interpolation}
vendor whose reconstructions a cross-vendor model is expected to
reproduce, while a large $\Delta$ marks an \textbf{extrapolation} vendor
whose records should anchor out-of-distribution claims and be scoped
cautiously. Each vendor's regime is labelled in the table from its
$\Delta$ against a deposit-fixed threshold, so a reuser can pick
interpolative vendors for in-distribution benchmarks and extrapolative
vendors for robustness stress-tests without re-deriving anything. The
$\Delta$ values themselves are populated from the Phase-3 runs
(\autoref{tab:reconstruction_quality}'s generation), so this is the
framing under which those numbers are read, not a separate experiment.

\paragraph{Uncertainty--error agreement (central reuse validation).} The
corpus's distinctive reuse value is the paired (uncertainty, error)
records, so we validate it along three complementary axes rather than one.
\textbf{(i) Rank tracking:} the Spearman correlation between $\sigma(y)$
and $e(y)$, per pixel and aggregated per case
(\autoref{tab:uq_calibration}) --- does larger predicted uncertainty
co-occur with larger error? \textbf{(ii) Calibration magnitude:} a
reliability diagram of predicted $\sigma$ against observed RMS error per
$\sigma$-bin (\autoref{fig:reliability}), summarised by an
expected-calibration-error (ECE), the mean absolute gap between the two ---
rank correlation can be high while the variance is systematically mis-scaled,
which ECE catches. \textbf{(iii) Interval coverage:} the empirical fraction
of voxels whose true error falls within $\pm k\,\sigma$ for $k \in
\{1, 2, 3\}$ (\autoref{tab:uq_coverage}); a well-calibrated Gaussian-like
variance yields $\approx 68/95/99.7\%$. Reporting all three lets a reuser
distinguish a uncertainty map that merely \emph{ranks} error from one that
is \emph{quantitatively} calibrated for interval-based triage; where they
disagree, the corpus documents which property holds so the variance is not
over-trusted. This is the single most important validation for the corpus's
stated calibration-research reuse.

\section{Usage Notes}
\label{sec:usage}

\paragraph{Loading.} All image records are NIfTI and load with any
standard reader (\texttt{nibabel}, ITK); credentials are JSON validated
against the published schema~\citep{ws2framework}. The
\texttt{all\_credentials.jsonl} index supports tabular queries by vendor,
dose, task, and verdict. Each record's \texttt{scan\_meta.json} links back
to its WS-1 raw source~\citep{ws1dataset}. A minimal read of one record's
voxel-aligned reconstruction, uncertainty, and error maps:

\begin{verbatim}
import nibabel as nib, json
base = "reconstructions/Siemens/<scan_id>/r025/"
recon = nib.load(base + "recon_mean.nii.gz").get_fdata()        # HU
sigma = nib.load(base + "uncertainty_sigma.nii.gz").get_fdata() # HU, same grid
error = nib.load(base + "error_abs.nii.gz").get_fdata()         # |recon - full_dose|
meta  = json.load(open(base + "scan_meta.json"))                # vendor, r, anatomy, ...
# the (sigma, error) pair is the ready-made calibration benchmark
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Intended reuses.} (1) Uncertainty-calibration research: treat
the paired (\texttt{uncertainty\_sigma}, \texttt{error\_abs}) maps as a
ready calibration benchmark. (2) Cross-vendor robustness: use vendor
labels and the leave-one-vendor-out validation to scope claims. (3)
Dose-dependent downstream-task studies: use the released task-score maps
without re-deriving reconstructions. (4) Method comparison: benchmark a
new reconstruction against the matched baseline records on identical
inputs and report a credential against the same framework hash.

\paragraph{Cautions for reuse.} The released uncertainty is ensemble
disagreement; it can be non-informative where all members fail in
correlated ways (out-of-distribution anatomy)---inspect the reliability
diagram before triaging on $\sigma$. Cross-vendor records for vendors not
in training are extrapolation, not measurement. Dose ratios below $0.10$
are not characterised. Reconstructions are single-slice 2D. Credential
\texttt{INDETERMINATE} verdicts on this cohort reflect sample
size~\citep{ws2framework}, not evidence of non-equivalence, and should not
be read as a negative finding about a method.

\paragraph{Licensing.} Numerical records (credentials, metadata,
manifest): CC~BY~4.0. LIDC-derived pixel records: CC~BY~3.0. Pixel records
derived from the DUA-restricted AAPM~2016 and Mayo LDCT-PD scans are not
redistributed and are regenerated locally via the RunBundle (see
\emph{Licensing and redistribution} under Data Records,
\autoref{sec:datarecords}). Generating code and RunBundle: Apache~2.0.
Reuse requires citing this Data Descriptor and the WS-1 source
dataset~\citep{ws1dataset}.

\section{Code Availability}
\label{sec:codeavail}

The complete generating pipeline---training, inference, ensemble
aggregation, error/task/credential emission, and the evaluation harness
that produced every Technical Validation number---is released under
Apache~2.0 at
\url{https://github.com/integritynoble/low_dose_CT/tree/main/WS-3_reference_method},
and packaged as a pinned Docker RunBundle; integrity is provided by the
deposited SHA-256 \texttt{MANIFEST.sha256}, with an optional
content-addressed mirror (IPFS CID \todo{at deposit}) as a supplementary
check. Pre-trained ensemble weights,
the pinned \texttt{Dockerfile}, evaluation scripts, random seeds, and the
training-compute disclosure (Supplementary Table~S3) permit bit-identical
regeneration of the corpus within floating-point tolerance on equivalent
hardware. The forward model is the upstream
\texttt{pwm\_core.contrib.modalities.ct\_radon}~\citep{pwmcore}; the
credentialing library is \texttt{pwm\_dose\_equivalence}~\citep{ws2framework}.
The credential-emission and corpus-packaging steps (record layout,
\texttt{pwm-audit}ed credential issuance, machine-readable metadata, and the
SHA-256 integrity manifest) are unit-tested, and a runnable synthetic fixture
reproduces the full record-generation $\rightarrow$ credential $\rightarrow$
metadata $\rightarrow$ manifest pipeline end-to-end on miniature data, so a
reviewer can exercise the corpus structure and audit chain in one command
without the full dataset.
